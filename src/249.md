## 1. Mathematical Foundations of the Octonionic Substrate

The search for the algebraic underpinnings of fundamental physical fields centers upon the classification of real normed division algebras. Hurwitz’s theorem of 1898 establishes that there exist precisely four such algebras over the field of real numbers: the real numbers ($\mathbb{R}$), the complex numbers ($\mathbb{C}$), the quaternions ($\mathbb{H}$), and the octonions ($\mathbb{O}$) [^1] [^2] [^28]. The Cayley–Dickson construction demonstrates how each successive algebra emerges through a doubling of dimension, with each algorithmic transition systematically sacrificing an algebraic property [^1] [^2]. Moving from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{C}$ discards total algebraic ordering [^1]. The progression from $\mathbb{C}$ to $\mathbb{H}$ preserves associativity while sacrificing commutativity [^1] [^2]. The final terminal step from $\mathbb{H}$ to the eight-dimensional octonions $\mathbb{O}$ forfeits associativity, rendering the octonions both non-commutative and non-associative [^1] [^2]. Beyond eight dimensions, the sedenions ($\mathbb{S}$) lose the division property entirely due to the presence of non-trivial zero divisors, establishing the octonions as the structural ceiling for normed division algebras [^1] [^2] [^3].

An element of the octonion algebra $x \in \mathbb{O}$ is formulated as a linear combination over eight real basis elements:

$$x = x_0 e_0 + \sum_{i=1}^7 x_i e_i$$

where $e_0 \equiv 1$ constitutes the real identity scalar and $\{e_1, \dots, e_7\}$ represent imaginary basis units [^1] [^2]. The multiplication kinematics of these imaginary units are dictated by the projective geometry of the seven-point Fano plane, satisfying the anti-commutation rule $e_i e_j + e_j e_i = -2\delta_{ij}$ for all distinct non-zero indices [^2] [^4] [^5]. Because the octonions fail the associative law, their triple products depend explicitly upon the ordering of parentheses, giving rise to a non-zero associator:

$$[x, y, z] \equiv (xy)z - x(yz) \neq 0$$

for arbitrary non-zero elements [^1] [^2] [^24]. The algebra retains the weaker property of alternativity, wherein the associator is completely alternating under any permutation of its three arguments [^1] [^2]. By Artin’s theorem, any subalgebra generated by any two arbitrary elements of $\mathbb{O}$ is strictly associative, isomorphic to a subalgebra of $\mathbb{H}$ [^1] [^2]. Non-zero octonions do not constitute a group under multiplication; instead, they generate a non-associative Moufang loop governed by the Moufang identities:

$$(ux)(yu) = u(xy)u$$

for all $u, x, y \in \mathbb{O}$ [^1] [^2] [^5]. The continuous automorphisms preserving the non-associative octonionic product form the compact exceptional Lie group $G_2$, a 14-dimensional manifold that anchors the exceptional Lie family: $F_4$, $E_6$, $E_7$, and $E_8$ [^1] [^2] [^26].

Geoffrey Dixon extended this architecture into the unified algebra $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R} \otimes \mathbb{C} \otimes \mathbb{H} \otimes \mathbb{O}$, mapping the tensor product of the four division algebras directly to the full gauge symmetries of the Standard Model [^3] [^26] [^27]. 

Cohl Furey resolved the operational difficulties of octonionic non-associativity by analyzing sequences of left-multiplication endomorphisms:

## 2. Subtractive Perception, Active Inference, and the Interface Model

The view that biological perception functions as an eliminative sieve rather than an accumulative camera originated in the metaphysics of Henri Bergson [^6]. In *Matter and Memory*, Bergson argued that perception is essentially subtractive: the living body isolates from the infinite totality of physical relations only those that directly concern its immediate capacity for biological action [^6]. The central nervous system serves not as a manufacturer of images, but as an instrument of selection and elimination [^6] [^7]. 

Aldous Huxley generalized this insight into the metaphor of the cerebral reducing valve, postulating that the living organism is embedded within an unbracketed, high-dimensional totality ("Mind at Large") [^6] [^7] [^8]. If admitted directly to conscious awareness, this torrent of undifferentiated information would paralyze the biological organism [^6] [^7]. To ensure biological survival, the nervous system acts as a choke point, shutting out the vast majority of the field and admitting only a trickle of heavily edited, biologically salient signals [^6] [^7].

Contemporary computational neuroscience formalizes the reducing valve through Karl Friston’s Free Energy Principle and the active inference framework [^7] [^9] [^11]. A biological organism maintains its structural and thermodynamic non-equilibrium steady state by minimizing variational free energy, an informational upper bound on surprisal [^9] [^11] [^12]:

$$F = \mathbb{E}_{q(\eta)} \left[ \ln q(\eta) - \ln p(\eta, s) \right] = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(q(\eta) \,||\, p(\eta | s)\right) - \ln p(s)$$

where $\eta$ represents external hidden states of the world, $s$ represents sensory inputs, and $q(\eta)$ denotes the internal recognition density over the hidden states [^9]. 

This relationship is sustained through the statistical topology of a Markov blanket [^9] [^10] [^12]. The blanket formalizes the conditional independence between internal states $\mu$ and external states $\eta$ by sandwiching them between active states $a$ and sensory states $s$ [^9] [^11] [^12]. Because the internal neural architecture possesses no direct access to the hidden causes generating its boundary perturbations, perception cannot operate as an exteroceptive recording device [^9] [^10]. Instead, the brain functions as a hierarchical predictive engine [^10] [^23]. It projects top-down generative predictions inward onto the sensory boundary, using bottom-up ascending signals exclusively to calculate prediction error vectors [^9] [^10] [^23]. What is perceived as an external, three-dimensional physical landscape is an inward, top-down projection—a controlled virtual reality maintained by predictive coding to preserve homeostatic viability [^6] [^7] [^9].

Donald Hoffman’s Interface Theory of Perception and the mathematically proven Fitness-Beats-Truth theorem complete this reduction [^13] [^14] [^15]. Using evolutionary game theory, Hoffman demonstrated that natural selection favors perceptual mechanisms tuned entirely to fitness payoffs over those tuned to veridical tracking of objective reality [^13] [^15]. In this formulation, three-dimensional Euclidean space and linear physical time act as an evolutionary desktop user interface, while perceived physical objects function as icons [^13]. The icon hides the complex physical field generating the interaction, providing the organism with a simplified, low-dimensional, actionable representation that minimizes cognitive overhead during fight-or-flight scenarios [^13] [^14]. The sensory apparatus compresses high-dimensional reality into an associative, computationally tractable spatial model [^13].

## 3. Somatic Resonance and the Primordial Affective Ground

While exteroceptive modalities project an actionable three-dimensional interface, the organism receives an independent stream of signaling from its interior: interoception [^16] [^18]. Antonio Damasio establishes that consciousness and identity do not originate within high-level neocortical deliberation, but within a layered hierarchy of somatic representation [^17] [^18] [^19]. At the base of this architecture lies the proto-self, an ensemble of pre-conscious neural patterns located in ancient subcortical brainstem structures, including the periaqueductal gray, the parabrachial nucleus, and the nucleus of the solitary tract [^19] [^20]. These deep nuclei continuously monitor the biochemical, vascular, and visceral state of the organism [^18] [^19].

This subcortical mapping generates what Damasio terms primordial feelings [^16] [^19]. Primordial feelings provide a direct, wordless, and unadorned experience of the living body's current homeostatic status [^19]. Unlike exteroceptive sensations, which present clear spatial edges and localized Cartesian coordinates, primordial somatic feelings manifest as background feelings [^17] [^18] [^19]. They reflect the holistic vitality of the visceral milieu, forming an affective background that colors the entire field of awareness [^17] [^19]. The subtle "dim signals" of existential peace or profound, unlocalized sorrow represent the unadorned resonance of this homeostatic ground state breaking through into awareness [^18] [^19].

The ascending neuroanatomical pathway for this somatic baseline is the lamina I spinothalamocortical system, mapped in detail by A.D. Craig [^18]. While classical somatosensation relies on large-diameter, myelinated fibers projecting to the primary somatosensory cortex, interoceptive information is transduced by small-diameter unmyelinated ($\mathrm{C}$) and thinly myelinated ($\mathrm{A}\delta$) primary afferents that terminate in every tissue of the body [^18]. These fibers register vascular smooth muscle tone, cardiorespiratory dynamics, pH, glucose levels, and immune cytokines [^18]. 

These interoceptive signals ascend through spinal lamina I to the homeostatic centers of the brainstem and the ventromedial thalamus, projecting directly to the dorsal posterior insular cortex [^18]. Within the insular cortex, a posterior-to-anterior computational progression re-represents these raw visceral inputs, integrating them with emotional, social, and cognitive context [^18]. 

The anterior insular cortex serves as the neuroanatomical hub for human self-awareness, producing what Craig defines as the conscious "material me" [^18]. Under ordinary evolutionary conditions, the high-definition sensory processing of the neocortex suppresses this subtle interoceptive baseline to prioritize external threat detection and motor execution [^6] [^18]. The physical body, perceived as a pumping heart and vascular circulation, is the primary biological instantiation of this field ($self$), persisting beneath the high-bandwidth exteroceptive projection [^18] [^19].

## 4. Dimensional Loss, Associative Bracketing, and the Origin of Time

The conceptual hypothesis proposes that sensory perception strips five dimensions from an eight-dimensional octonionic base ($8 - 5 = 3$), leaving a three-dimensional spatial manifold and giving rise to the arrow of time as the sum total of the discarded degrees of freedom. This operation can be evaluated through the lens of non-associative geometry, the algebraic physics of time, and cognitive segmentation theory.

The algebraic consequence of operating within a non-associative algebra is the mathematical necessity of parenthesization [^1] [^2]. In an associative algebra, an expression such as $a \cdot b \cdot c \cdot d$ is unambiguous; the internal grouping of terms can be shifted arbitrarily without altering the result [^1]. In a non-associative algebra, an unbracketed string of elements is mathematically undefined: $(a(bc))d \neq ((ab)c)d \neq (ab)(cd)$ [^1] [^2]. In algebraic topology and operad theory, the geometric space parameterizing every distinct parenthesization of an $n$-element non-associative product forms a convex polytope termed the Stasheff associahedron ($K_n$) [^24] [^25]. 

For $n = 3$, the associahedron $K_3$ is a 1-dimensional line segment joining the two bracketings $(ab)c$ and $a(bc)$, with the segment representing the associator isomorphism [^24]. For $n = 4$, the associahedron $K_4$ forms a 2-dimensional pentagon whose five vertices denote the five unique ways to parenthesize four variables, connected by edges representing single associative shifts [^24]. For general $n$, $K_n$ is a polytope of dimension $n - 2$ [^24] [^25]. 

When an intrinsically non-associative field is coarse-grained into an associative representation space, the intrinsic ambiguity of unordered products must be resolved [^3]. Bracketing is the operational imposition of a causal pathway: it forces the system to select an ordered, sequential trajectory across the associahedral complex [^24]. Parenthesization generates syntactic structure, discrete causal progression, and conceptual abstraction [^24] [^25]. It converts a continuous, non-associative field into an ordered linear sequence [^3].

The physical mechanism linking dimensional reduction, information loss, and the arrow of time is formalized in the Thermal Time Hypothesis developed by Alain Connes and Carlo Rovelli [^21] [^22]. In generally covariant physical theories, such as general relativity and loop quantum gravity, coordinate time is a gauge variable rather than a fundamental physical parameter; the dynamic Wheeler–DeWitt equation $\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle = 0$ contains no universal time parameter [^21] [^22]. 

Connes and Rovelli established that a macroscopic time flow emerges exclusively when an observer or subsystem coarse-grains a microscopic operator algebra [^21] [^22]. Given a von Neumann algebra of observables $\mathcal{M}$ and a statistical state or density matrix $\rho$, the Tomita–Takesaki theorem demonstrates that any faithful normal state over $\mathcal{M}$ canonically generates a one-parameter group of modular automorphisms $\alpha_t^\rho \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})$:

$$\alpha_t^\rho(A) = \Delta^{it} A \Delta^{-it} \quad \forall A \in \mathcal{M}, \; t \in \mathbb{R}$$

where $\Delta$ is the positive, self-adjoint modular operator determined by the state [^22]. The thermal time hypothesis postulates that physical time flow is identical to this modular flow:

$$t_{\mathrm{thermal}} = -\frac{1}{\beta} \ln \rho$$

Time is the parameter of an observer’s statistical ignorance [^21] [^22]. If an observer had access to the fine-grained microscopic state across all dimensions, no thermodynamic dissipation would occur, and no macroscopic arrow of time would emerge [^21] [^22]. 

When five dimensions are projected out to render a three-dimensional spatial manifold, an immense volume of physical phase space is discarded into an unobserved statistical reservoir [^9] [^22]. By Liouville's theorem and the von Neumann entropy relation $S = -\mathrm{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho)$, this severe dimensional truncation produces an irreversible entropy gradient [^22]. The resulting thermodynamic dissipation is experienced phenomenologically as the relentless, irreversible passage of time—an arrow of time that sensory reality cannot touch because it is the statistical consequence of the filtering process itself [^21] [^22].

The perceptual system maintains an active internal representation called an Event Model in working memory to predict immediate sensory changes [^23]. As long as predictions match incoming sensory data, the event model remains stable, and time is perceived as smooth and continuous [^23]. When a significant shift occurs in the sensory environment, prediction error surges [^23]. This error spike is broadcast throughout the cortical hierarchy, forcing a reset of the active event model [^23]. 

This transient reset is perceived as an event boundary [^23]. These boundaries slice the continuous flux of physical reality into discrete cognitive "chapters," allowing memory to encode experience into seconds, minutes, days, and historical epochs [^23]. Time, at the level of cognitive intelligence, is an apparatus of abstract bracketing engineered by prediction error dynamics [^23].

## 5. Critical Synthesis and Epistemic Boundaries

A rigorous evaluation of the proposed framework requires identifying where mathematical physics and neurobiology substantiate the conceptual model, and where literal interpretations encounter physical and physiological limits.

The conceptual model aligns with established science across four core domains. First, the inward projection of reality matches the computational architecture of predictive processing and active inference: perception is an internally generated simulation constrained by sensory prediction errors, rather than a direct exteroceptive capture of an external world [^6] [^9]. Second, Damasio’s identification of the proto-self and primordial feelings confirms that the deepest layer of consciousness is subcortical, visceral, and interoceptive, operating as a continuous background affective tone beneath the high-bandwidth exteroceptive interface [^18] [^19]. Third, the derivation of time from discarded degrees of freedom is supported by the Connes–Rovelli Thermal Time Hypothesis, which proves that macroscopic time flow arises directly from the statistical coarse-graining of microscopic operator algebras [^21] [^22]. Fourth, the concept of abstraction as bracketing reflects the topological geometry of the Stasheff associahedron, where non-associative structures require parenthesization to generate linear, sequential, and causal operational meaning [^3] [^24].

However, critical boundaries emerge when evaluating the literal mechanics of the hypothesis. The arithmetic subtraction of five sensory modalities from an eight-dimensional base ($8 - 5 = 3$) functions as an evocative heuristic rather than a formal projection operator in differential geometry. In mathematical physics, dimensional reduction operates via rigorous manifold fibrations or compactifications, such as Kaluza–Klein or Calabi–Yau fibrations $\mathcal{M}^8 \to \mathcal{M}^3 \times \mathcal{K}^5$. The human sensory apparatus does not project onto canonical orthogonal basis vectors $\{e_3, e_4, e_5, e_6, e_7\}$; each sensory modality is a complex, multi-channel transducer operating across continuous frequency spectra and spatial distributions.

Furthermore, the biological classification of human sensation into precisely five senses is an Aristotelian taxonomy superseded by modern physiology. Human perception incorporates multiple distinct, independent sensory systems that are neither reducible to the classical five nor purely exteroceptive. These include the vestibular system (transducing angular acceleration and gravity), proprioception (muscle spindles and Golgi tendon organs mapping limb position), thermoception, nociception, and visceral interoceptors (baroreceptors, chemoreceptors, and osmoreceptors) [^18]. If each sensory modality stripped away an independent spatial dimension, the human sensorium would project away far more than five dimensions.

Finally, neural computation operates within associative formal systems [^3]. Biological neural networks perform spatial summation, temporal integration, and tensor operations that rely on associative matrix multiplications [^3]. In physical theories where octonions are utilized, such as Furey’s particle representations, the non-associative algebra is mapped into the associative Clifford algebra $C\ell(6)$ via left-multiplication endomorphisms to yield well-defined linear operators and observable eigenvalues [^5] [^26] [^27]. While non-associative algebras offer a compelling model for pre-bracketed physical reality, the biological brain itself processes information through associative neural representations [^3] [^27].

## 6. Conclusions

The hypothesis that intelligence is an engine of abstraction operating by projecting an actionable three-dimensional associative reality from an eight-dimensional non-associative somatic field provides a compelling synthesis across modern scientific disciplines. Grounding perception in an inward-projecting generative model aligns with Karl Friston’s Free Energy Principle and Donald Hoffman’s Interface Theory of Perception: three-dimensional space is not an exhaustive physical container, but an evolutionary user interface engineered to optimize biological fitness and enable motor action [^9] [^13]. The subtle, unlocalized background signals of peace and deep sorrow correspond to Antonio Damasio’s primordial feelings and A.D. Craig’s subcortical interoceptive pathways—the unbracketed homeostatic hum of the living visceral body ($self$) persisting beneath the high-bandwidth exteroceptive interface [^18] [^19].

The integration of algebraic non-associativity with thermodynamics elucidates the emergence of the arrow of time. As established by the Connes–Rovelli Thermal Time Hypothesis, time is the mathematical consequence of statistical coarse-graining: when higher degrees of freedom are projected out, entropy increases, generating the macroscopic thermodynamic arrow [^21] [^22]. Concurrently, the non-associativity of the underlying field requires the cognitive apparatus to impose parentheses—an associahedral bracketing operation that manifests psychologically through Event Segmentation Theory [^23] [^24]. Intelligence abstracts by bracketing, slicing continuous, non-associative physical totality into the discrete events, historical chapters, and temporal narratives necessary for mortal organisms to navigate a perilous universe [^23] [^24]. While the literal mapping of five sensory organs to five octonionic dimensions represents a poetic idealization rather than a strict neuroanatomical isomorphism, the broader paradigm stands as a profound conceptual framework unifying non-associative mathematics, biological filtering, somatic interoception, and the emergent nature of time.
